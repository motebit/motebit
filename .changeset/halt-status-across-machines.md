`halt-status` is asked of every machine, and the answers are composed rather than raced.

`halt-status` is the verb whose job is answering "did my motebit stop", and the halt store it reads is per machine. The relay delivered it first-wins, so on a motebit with a daemon on a laptop and a worker on a VPS it answered from whichever machine the relay happened to pick — "Running — nothing is halted" about a motebit whose other machine was stopped, or a stop reported for one still working elsewhere. #686 pointed a consumer at `halt-status` for the per-machine truth and then had to take the pointer back, because it was not yet true (issue #687).

Now a many-machine `halt-status` is delivered **once per machine** — not per connection: `motebit run` and `motebit serve` on a host share a device id and a replay guard, and the envelope is single-use, so a second frame is refused as a replay by its own motebit — and the relay answers with one line per machine, each under its device id, each carrying that machine's own reply verbatim.

- **The relay does not add the answers up.** It knows what it sent and what came back. It does not know what a goal is or which machine owns one, and the withdrawn broadcast's AND across machines was false about two ordinary cases (#681). The only verdict it publishes is about the picture — `data.partial` — never about the interior.
- **A machine that did not report is named, and how is kept apart:** `unreached` (no open socket, nothing sent), `silent` (delivered, nothing back by the deadline), `no_record` (it replied without a record — a replayed envelope refused by its guard, a surface with no halt store). "1 runtime did not answer" tells a sovereign something may still be running and not where.
- **A partial picture is not a 2xx.** Honest prose at HTTP 200 still lets `motebit halt-status --remote && <next>` proceed on half an answer, so a partial is a 504 carrying the composed body. Nothing heard at all stays a 504 _with_ a body — composing an empty list once produced a 200 with no body and turned the CLI's timeout branch into a JSON parse error. Nothing delivered at all is a 404, as it is for one machine.
- **Machines are counted, not answers.** A line is filled once, by the connection it was delivered to: a repeat does not overwrite, and an answer from a machine never asked settles nothing. Attribution comes from the socket (`device_id` at upgrade), never from anything the answer says about itself — one runtime cannot speak in another's name.
- **One deadline.** No second, shorter "grace" after the first answer: the relay cannot compute how long a machine's interior takes, and two guesses at it were both wrong. It waits for every machine it reached and composes at the request's own deadline.
- **Both consumer surfaces move together**, through one reader. The truth of a partial lives in the body of an error response, which is where surfaces are least careful: each already keys copy on the status, and "Delivered, but the runtime did not answer" is false about a machine that was never reached. Last time the terminal learned this and the phone did not. `readComposedCommandResult` lives in `@motebit/runtime` beside `CommandResult`; `motebit halt-status --remote` and the phone's `/halted` both call it before they look at the status, print the per-machine report, and still fail — exit 1, and a thrown error — because half a picture is not a result.

Undeclared device ids fall back to first-wins, as they do for every other verb here: the relay invents one per connection, so grouping by it would read one host's two processes as two machines. A one-machine motebit — every deployment that exists until the installer ships (#685) — gets its runtime's own reply, untouched, and tests assert it.

**Why the read before the act.** Composing a halt (#681) needs five things; a read needs three of them — per-machine delivery, a partial-result status, and sibling surfaces moving together — and avoids the two that made the broadcast hard (no grace window is computable for stoppers that take up to 10s; no verdict has to be synthesized, because the list _is_ the answer). So that machinery is proven here, on a verb that is safe to ask twice, against two real runtimes in the multi-runtime harness; every claim above was written as a failing test and tamper-checked by breaking the production line it protects. `halt` and `resume` on many machines are still refused. #681 adds the act on top of machinery that is no longer new.

The relay's WebSocket handler had no test of its own — the harness's note that "the relay's own tests cover it" was not true — so `websocket-command-response.test.ts` now drives the real handler for the one fact the harness has to take on trust: who an answer is from.
