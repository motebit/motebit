"@motebit/relay": patch
---

An x402-funded task submission is credited with its own settlement, once, to the principal who paid, and a same-key replay is never charged (#907, #925; `spec/delegation-v1.md` §3.3, 1.5; `spec/market-v1.md` §11.2).

`@x402/hono`'s middleware with `ExactEvmScheme` (eip3009) settles AFTER the handler, and only on a response below 400. The relay captured the settle tx hash in an `onAfterSettle` hook into a variable shared by every request, and the handler read it before its own settlement existed. Verified with the real library (probe `x402-settlement-907.probe.ts`, differential vs main):

- the paying request was refused (402), so it was never settled and x402 funding never worked;
- a paid same-key replay of an admitted task was charged onchain and credited nowhere (#925);
- the hash it left behind was credited as a `deposit` to the NEXT submission, including another principal's (#907).

Client-visible changes:

- **The payment is settled by the request that admits it.** The gate now uses the library's manual API: it verifies the payment (`processHTTPRequest`) and binds it to the request, and the handler settles it (the resource server's `settlePayment`, called directly so its thrown errors can be classified) once, after every pre-admission refusal and immediately before admission. No settlement happens after the handler. A refused request is not charged: its EIP-3009 authorization is never submitted and expires at its `validBefore`. A successful submission carries the `PAYMENT-RESPONSE` header.
- **x402 pays the relay treasury.** The challenge's `payTo` is `x402Config.payToAddress`, not the worker's `pay_to_address`. The relay credits the authorization's value — which must equal the quoted gross — to the delegator's account and pays the worker's account at settlement. Before, the worker was paid onchain and again from the delegator's account, and the relay was out the price. The x402 gate still arms only for a listing that publishes a `pay_to_address`.
- **Funding is decided once.** A verified payment funds its task. The hold is capped at that payment, so the 1.2× buffer never draws on the delegator's other funds, including another request's concurrent x402 deposit. Without a payment the virtual account funds the task.
- **Replay and conflict are never priced.** A request whose `Idempotency-Key` already holds a claim is served by the idempotency record: never challenged, verified or charged. An unpaid same-key replay of an admitted task now gets the replay (201), not a 402.
- **Residual, unchanged from #901:** a paid request whose delegator's escrow hold nets the new deposit below the price is charged, credited, and refused `402 INSUFFICIENT_FUNDS` naming the credited payment (withdrawable once the hold clears).
- **An unknown settle outcome is never reported as "not charged" (round 2).** Before the facilitator is called, the relay writes a `pending` record keyed by the EIP-3009 authorization (payer, nonce) — migration v49, `relay_x402_settlements`, declared in the transparency declaration and PRIVACY.md. The answer is classified in a closed set:
  - settled (success with a tx hash on this network, and — if the facilitator reports an amount — exactly the authorized value, strictly parsed) ⇒ the authorization's value credited once to the delegator;
  - a definite pre-broadcast refusal (a closed set of the facilitator's `invalid_exact_evm_*` verification reasons, `insufficient_funds`, …, with no transaction hash) ⇒ `402 TASK_X402_SETTLEMENT_FAILED` "nothing has been charged" — and, since the signed authorization stays executable until `validBefore`, "if it is executed anyway, the payment is credited to account <delegator>" (round 7: the message no longer overclaims);
  - anything else — a timeout, a non-JSON or 5xx answer, a network error, `FacilitatorResponseError`, a refusal reason outside the set, `invalid_exact_evm_nonce_already_used` (used OR cancelled), `invalid_exact_evm_transaction_failed` (the fallback `@x402/evm` v2's `parseEip3009TransferError` returns for any unrecognised thrown settle error, including a receipt wait that timed out after broadcast), a reverted or event-mismatched transaction, a refusal carrying a transaction hash, a success on another network or for another amount, a crash — ⇒ `402 TASK_X402_OUTCOME_UNKNOWN` "Do NOT pay again", with `x402_settlement` {payer, nonce, amount_micro, delegator, network, status, valid_before} in the body, logged at error.
- **Reconciliation credits only on proof of execution (round 3).** A supervised loop (`x402-reconciliation`, every 60 s, `MOTEBIT_X402_RECONCILIATION_INTERVAL_MS`) reads the token contract's EIP-3009 EVENTS for the record's (payer, nonce) — never the `authorizationState` bit, which USDC sets on `cancelAuthorization` too (a payer could cancel between verify and settle and be credited for nothing):
  - an `AuthorizationUsed(payer, nonce)` log AND the Transfer the token emitted for it — the NEXT log after that Used log in the same transaction receipt, from the same token contract (both located in the receipt's log array, so a node's block- or tx-scoped `logIndex` cannot mis-pair them; round 5) (Circle stablecoin-evm `EIP3009._transferWithAuthorization` emits `AuthorizationUsed` in `_markAuthorizationAsUsed` and then `Transfer` in `_transfer`, nothing between) — from the payer to the treasury for exactly the recorded amount, in a successful receipt ⇒ credited once, recording the tx hash and consuming that Transfer log (`(tx_hash, credit_log_index)` is unique: one Transfer log never credits two records). Round 4: an earlier version accepted ANY matching Transfer in the transaction, so one batch transaction burning N nonces to self plus one Transfer to the treasury credited N× the payment. A used authorization whose paired Transfer does not match ⇒ `failed execution_mismatch`, logged at error for the operator. Every log field (emitting address, topics, `removed`, block range) is re-checked locally, never trusted to the node's filter;
  - an `AuthorizationCanceled(payer, nonce)` log ⇒ `failed authorization_cancelled` (never credited);
  - neither, the scan complete to the confirmed head, and that head's OWN block timestamp past `validBefore` + 120 s — observed TWICE, at least 5 minutes apart, the second a full rescan from the start — ⇒ `failed authorization_expired_unused` (one lagging read never expires a record);
  - otherwise pending; any read error leaves it pending.

  Bounds (round 6): every scan ends at a FIXED block — the first block whose timestamp is past `validBefore` + 120 s (binary search; EIP-3009 cannot execute after it) — so a completed scan is a stable fact however far the head has moved; the ordinary pass and each full pass (the confirming observation, every re-check) keep persisted cursors, so a range longer than one run's budget advances across runs; a re-check is spent only when its pass concludes; records are visited least-recently-checked first (round 8 made this a true FIFO; see below). Round 7: every re-check eligibility condition — including chain time past `validBefore`, compared against the confirmed head's timestamp passed in as a bind parameter — is applied in SQL before the LIMIT (100 cheap still-valid refusals had held every slot); the reconciliation loop is single-flight; every cursor and pass write is a compare-and-set on the status, the pass generation and the cursor value the run observed, so a stale concurrent writer is a no-op; `eth_getLogs` pages halve on a provider's range-limit error. Before round 6, a reconciler gap over ~28 h left records pending forever (the confirming rescan chased the moving head) and 100 of them starved every newer one. Reads never go past the confirmed head (`latest − confirmations`, the deposit detector's per-chain depth); `eth_getLogs` is filtered by both indexed topics and paged in 2 000-block ranges, at most 25 pages per record per run, from the newest block at or before the authorization's signed `validAfter` to the confirmed head, with the cursor persisted. A `failed` record whose reason is a facilitator refusal or an expiry is re-checked up to three times (a full rescan each: 10 min after it failed, then 1 h, then 6 h — chain time, see round 10), once its `validBefore` has passed by the chain's clock, and credited if an execution turns up — a failure is not terminal against evidence; an `execution_mismatch` is re-checked once (a node quirk). The scan's first block is found by binary search over block timestamps (O(log n) reads), so a record first reconciled long after it was created (a freeze, an RPC outage, a reader enabled late) is still searched from the start of its window — round 5: a capped linear walk persisted a too-late start and missed the payment. The settling request that learns its record left `pending` while its settle was in flight funds the task only if the record is `credited`; anything else is `402 TASK_X402_OUTCOME_UNKNOWN`, never admitted on the account's other funds.

- **Selection is FIFO with a bounded wait (round 8).** Each run takes records in `COALESCE(last_checked_at, created_at)` order, then by `created_at` (index `idx_x402_settlements_selection` on exactly that expression). Both stamps are minted inside the write that uses them as `max(now, largest queued key + 1)` (round 9), so they strictly increase across processes and restarts. A record visited at time t therefore sorts behind everything created or visited after t, so it is revisited within ceil(records with a smaller key / limit) + 1 runs, however many new records arrive. Before round 8, the order was `COALESCE(last_checked_at, 0)`: every new record sorted ahead of every visited one, so a steady stream of new records starved a visited record forever. An `execution_mismatch` now has its own one-re-check budget (`mismatch_rechecks`). A mismatch found by an expiry re-check that used the last expiry re-check still gets that one mismatch re-check.
- **Operating cost:** `relay_x402_settlements` records are never pruned (they are the ledger of the authorizations the relay settled). Each new record costs about 25 to 50 RPC reads: the binary searches for its scan's start and end blocks, plus its `eth_getLogs` pages. A public RPC's rate limit lowers the rate of records the loop can serve before older records wait longer. Production operators should set a keyed RPC, `X402_RPC_URL_<NETWORK>` (see the operator troubleshooting doc).
- **The relay's wall clock never defines the scanned window or the queue order (round 9).**
  - The scan covers exactly the authorization's SIGNED window: it starts at the last block whose timestamp is at or before `validAfter` (EIP-3009 executes only when `validAfter < block.timestamp < validBefore`) and ends at the first block past `validBefore` + 120 s. Before round 9 the start was `created_at` − 1 h by the relay's clock. With that clock more than an hour fast, the start landed after the execution block, and an executed payment was marked `authorization_expired_unused` and never credited — the operator resolve could not recover it.
  - The gate refuses (`400 TASK_X402_PAYMENT_UNBOUND`, nothing settled) an authorization whose `validBefore − validAfter` is not positive or exceeds 7 200 s (`X402_MAX_WINDOW_SECONDS`), from the signed values alone, so a client choosing `validAfter = 0` cannot make the relay scan from genesis. x402 exact-EVM clients sign a 900 s window (`now − 600` to `now + 300`). The relay-clock bound below stays as a sanity check; with the relay's clock two hours slow it refuses the payment unsettled.
  - Queue stamps are seeded from the database, not a per-process counter, so a wall clock that jumps back across a restart (or two relay processes on one database) cannot leave stamps "in the future" and starve a record.
  - Every read stops at the current reader's confirmed head, and a pass concludes only when that head is at or past the fixed end: a load-balanced node whose head is behind a persisted end no longer "scans" blocks it does not have.
- **Every wait is measured in chain time (round 10).** The expiry confirmation gap and the re-check backoff compare the confirmed head's timestamp stored at each stamp (`expiry_observed_head_ts`, `resolved_head_ts`, `next_recheck_head_ts`, migration v49) with the current confirmed head's timestamp. A definite refusal decided on the request path, which holds no chain reader, is stamped with the head the loop first sees it under. A failed head read makes nothing eligible that run. The wall clock only ticks the loop. Before round 10, with the relay clock 30 days fast and then corrected, an executed payment waited about 30 days to be credited (the gap and backoff compared wall-clock stamps written while fast). The wall-clock columns remain, for the operator's eyes only.
- **The re-check budgets are orthogonal, whatever the record's origin (rounds 11, 13).** `failure_reason` keeps the record's ORIGINAL failure class and is never rewritten by a re-check. Every failed record has three expiry-class re-checks (10 min / 1 h / 6 h of chain time) and, once a paired mismatch has been seen, one mismatch re-check besides — a pending-origin mismatch included (round 13: it used to get only the one mismatch re-check). The first mismatch observation spends nothing; each LATER mismatch observation spends one (the mismatch re-check, then the expiry-class ones). **Terminal rule:** a record whose Used log paired to a wrong-value Transfer is terminal once both budgets are spent — after its first mismatch observation, (1 − mismatch re-checks spent) + (3 − expiry-class re-checks spent) further mismatch observations: 4 further (5 in all) when no expiry-class re-check was spent before the evidence, fewer when some were (a refused record read lagging twice, then with a wrong-value Transfer three times, closes after those 3). Reads that show nothing or miss the Transfer spend nothing, so they can neither close nor extend it. The same rule closes a CORRECT payment that a node misreports with a wrong-value Transfer that many times; the operator's resolve, a whole-window scan, still credits such a record. Every spend leaves a non-null chain-time wait.
- **Execution evidence is a sticky FLAG, never a pinned transaction (rounds 12–13).** Once a head-capped scan of the signed window has returned an `AuthorizationUsed(payer, nonce)` log for a record (`used_state`, migration v49), the record never becomes terminal from a read that shows nothing or a receipt missing its Transfer: it stays eligible, spends nothing, and is visited on a chain-time cadence (10 min, 1 h, then every 6 h) with no terminal give-up, so it is re-read about every 6 h until credited. **Every chain read the reconciler or the operator makes is a head-capped scan of the signed window at confirmed depth**; a receipt is read only for a Used log that same scan just returned, never by a stored hash (`used_observed_tx` is kept for the operator's eyes only). So the execution is found wherever it lands — in the transaction first seen or, after a reorg, in its re-inclusion T' — and nothing is ever credited from above the confirmed head. Round 12 re-read the first-seen transaction's receipt directly: a reorged or reverted T (read as `[]`) stranded the record forever — even the operator returned `used_unpaired` — and a re-inclusion above the confirmed head was credited. A scan that shows no Used after evidence was seen clears nothing: the evidence was read at confirmed depth, and a reorg deeper than the confirmation depth is **outside the stated threat model**. Before round 12, a pending record whose first Used read hit a node that dropped the Transfer log was marked `execution_mismatch`, and one later lagging read spent its only re-check.
- **A lost write race never discards an honest read (round 14).** When a run loses a cursor compare-and-set (another process moved the record on), it finishes its scan READ-ONLY up to the head instead of stopping; a Used log it finds may still be credited through the status-guarded credit path, and nothing else — it never spends a re-check, fails a record, records evidence or sets a visit cadence (not even on a read error), and it finishes past its page budget. Before, a lagging process that won every write race spent all three re-checks while the honest process never read its second page. (Production runs one process on SQLite; this is defence in depth.)
- **A stale pass never fails an evidenced record (round 14).** The expiry-unused and cancellation failures are written only to a record with no execution evidence (`used_state IS NULL`), so a pass that started before another run saw the Used log cannot fail it; such a blocked write is reported and counted as `unchanged`, never as expired or cancelled. Failing a pending record and flagging its mismatch evidence commit in one transaction.
- **One confirmed head per run (round 12).** Selection and every pass in a run use the head read once at its start; a second, regressed read can no longer change a pass's decision. A failed head read visits nothing (logged, counted).
- **A read error never makes a record hot (round 13).** Any read error on a visit still sets that record's next visit on the same chain-time cadence, so a failing record is not re-read every tick; the next good read of a record without execution evidence resets it. Loop errors are counted per record.
- **The operator's resolve reads the whole window in one call (round 12)**, from its start (never the loop's cursor), capped at the confirmed head, whatever the record's `used_state`, however many pages a provider's range cap forces; it reports `authorization_still_valid`, `no_execution_found`, `used_unpaired` or `execution_mismatch`, never `still_pending` for a failed record.
- **The x402 gate is never armed for a network the reconciler cannot read (round 11).** A relay configured with `x402.payToAddress` on a network with no RPC URL (`X402_RPC_URL_<NETWORK>` or a default) or no confirmation depth refuses to boot, instead of accepting payments whose unknown outcomes it could never prove.
- **Known gap, not fixed here (#971):** the treasury reconciliation still sums only recorded `platform_fee`, while an x402 payment's full gross now lands in the treasury, so it reports that difference as drift.
- **`validBefore` is bounded (round 4):** an authorization valid for more than 3 600 s from now is refused `400 TASK_X402_PAYMENT_UNBOUND` before settling, so a record's pending life and the reconciler's scan window stay bounded.
- **Operator note:** reconciliation selects records regardless of their `network`; an operator who switches the configured x402 network would have old records scanned on the new chain (they would find nothing and, without evidence, expire). Not handled here.
- **Operator door:** `GET /api/v1/admin/x402-settlements` (master token; `?status=pending|failed`) lists records; `POST /api/v1/admin/x402-settlements/:payer/:nonce/resolve` re-runs the proof-of-execution check for one record — it never credits without that proof and, unless it credits, writes nothing at all (round 11: no status, cursor, stamp, observation or budget change), and before `validBefore` (by chain time) reports `authorization_still_valid` instead of expiring anything. RPC per network: `X402_RPC_URL_<CAIP-2 upper, non-alphanumeric → _>` (e.g. `X402_RPC_URL_EIP155_8453`), else the default.
- **Same key while pending:** `409 TASK_X402_OUTCOME_PENDING` naming the record, before anything is priced — no second payment is settled. After reconciliation credits it, the same key is funded from the account.
- **Replay:** one signed authorization is settled at most once, under any key (`409 TASK_X402_PAYMENT_REPLAYED`), enforced by the (payer, nonce) primary key even against a facilitator that would settle it twice.
- **Binding:** a payment verified for one price is not settled for another — a listing repriced while the payment was at the facilitator is `409 TASK_X402_PAYMENT_UNBOUND`, nothing settled.
- A payment that is not an EIP-3009 authorization of exactly the quoted price to the treasury is refused `400 TASK_X402_PAYMENT_UNBOUND` before settling.
