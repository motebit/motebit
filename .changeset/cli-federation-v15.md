`motebit federation peer`, `mesh` and `peer-remove` speak relay-federation@1.5. A relay's handshake confirm is now minted by its own operator (self-propose is refused by 1.5 relays), so `peer` mints our confirm with our token and, when `MOTEBIT_PEER_API_TOKEN` holds the peer's master token, the peer's too; without it the peer holds us active and its operator runs `motebit federation peer <our-url>` for the other half. `mesh` takes one token per URL from `MOTEBIT_MESH_TOKENS` (comma-separated, in order), else the configured relay token for all. `peer-remove` reads the peer's relay id and asks our relay for a v2 `peer-remove` message bound to that one peer. Needs relays on relay-federation@1.5 (sibling changeset `relay-federation-v2-heartbeat-remove-ignored.md`). Each half now verifies the counterparty's v2 `peer-challenge` (from its key, to us, answering our own nonce, for our own endpoint and key) before asking our relay to mint anything, and the mint names the counterparty as recipient — by relay id AND by the public key its challenge verified under (relay ids are not self-certifying, so a counterparty claiming another relay's id under its own key gets a confirm that relay refuses) — and our own endpoint — never a bare nonce — so a counterparty that hands back another relay's nonce cannot carry our confirm there. Failure steps are named `<propose|challenge|operator-confirm|confirm>-<a-to-b|b-to-a>`.

`peer-remove` no longer reads the peer URL's `/identity`: it asks our relay to mint a removal for the confirmed peer our relay holds at that endpoint — addressed by the id and key our relay verified it under (relay ids are not self-certifying, so a counterparty claiming another peer's id could otherwise obtain a removal good at that peer) — and fails when our relay holds no confirmed peer there. `peer` verifies the challenge as addressed to our own key, and its confirm carries the key and endpoint our propose named.
