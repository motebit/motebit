Pairing binds the relay-supplied `motebit_id` to the transferred identity key — rotated identities included, whether or not the relay ever saw the rotation — refuses a key the relay's verified chain shows superseded or forked, refuses an approval whose key transfer is missing or does not verify, refuses non-canonical ids, and every identity-verify path (the canonical `motebit-verify` included) reads the succession chain.

`@motebit/crypto`:

- `verifyPairingIdentityBinding(motebitId, transferredPublicKeyHex, { successionSources?, guardianKey? })` — the binding ladder applied at pairing, offline, with no operator trust:
  - `motebitId` must be a canonical spelling: a lowercase UUIDv7, UUIDv8 or UUIDv4, or a `did:key`. Anything else (case-folded, braced, `urn:uuid:`-prefixed, hyphenless, padded, empty, a non-RFC variant, an unminted UUID version such as v1/v6, or free text) is refused (`malformed_id`), never normalized. UUIDv4 is accepted because desktop/mobile seed-only restore minted `crypto.randomUUID()` ids from 4d19695e (2026-05-15) until 6c0c710c (2026-05-22) and the relay admits them; refusing them locked those identities out of pairing;
  - the id is the self-certifying commitment to the key → accepted (`sovereign`);
  - a UUIDv8 / `did:key` whose CURRENT key (after rotation) is the transferred key → accepted (`sovereign`) only when the succession records connect them: every link verified, the chain continuous and strictly ordered in time, rooted at the key the id commits to, ending at the transferred key. `successionSources` are tried in order (records, or lazy loaders; at most 256 records read from any one source), each later one only when the records so far do not bind; records are self-verifying, so no source is trusted — a withheld, failed or forged chain only refuses;
  - a lineage that runs through a guardian-recovery link with no `guardianKey` pinned on the device → refused with `guardian_recovery_unverifiable` and a sentence saying why and what to do;
  - once a UUIDv8 / `did:key` binds, the relay's served chain (`relaySuccession`, a new option — fetched once, also the last fallback source) is ALWAYS fetched and checked against the lineage: a verified record in it that rotates the transferred key away (the relay chain extends the transfer's), or gives a key on the lineage a second successor (two different successors of one key — equivocation; the transfer's own records are checked for this too) → refused with `identity_fork`, `identity fork detected for motebit_id …`. Only verified records count, so a relay can prove a fork but never manufacture one; a relay chain that equals or is a prefix of the lineage (an offline rotation not yet uploaded) is no conflict;
  - a legacy UUIDv7 or UUIDv4 commits to no key → accepted at `unverified`, and the relay is not consulted.
- New exported types `PairingIdentityRefusalCode` (incl. `identity_fork`), `PairingSuccessionSource`, `PairingRelayCheck`; `PairingIdentityBindingResult` gains `code`, `relayCheck` (`no_conflict` / `unreachable` / `not_checked`) — how far the acceptance was checked against the relay, reported rather than implied — and `lineage`: on a `sovereign` acceptance, the VERIFIED links genesis → transferred key (empty when the id commits to the key itself), the only records a pairing device may persist.
- `identityVerifyOutcome(result)` — an identity file is intact only when its signature AND its succession chain verify.

`@motebit/protocol`: `KeyTransferPayload` gains three optional fields, present together or not at all — `encrypted_succession`, `succession_nonce`, `succession_tag`: Device A's own key-succession records, AES-256-GCM-sealed under the same derived transfer key as the seed. Additive and backward-compatible: a payload without them is the earlier shape; an earlier Device B ignores them; the relay stores the transfer opaquely and passes them through.

Pairing on mobile, desktop and web:

- Device A seals its own succession records into the key transfer (the bound identity file's chain and the roster replica's verified links). A sovereign identity rotated while no relay was configured — the relay's `/succession` is `[]` because nothing uploaded the rotation — now pairs; the previous revision of this change could never pair it.
- Device B runs one shared acceptance path (`openPairingKeyTransfer` in the private `@motebit/encryption`): the transfer is REQUIRED (none, undecryptable, or a seed that does not re-derive its `identity_pubkey_check` → refused), then the binding above — the transferred chain first, the relay's public `GET /api/v1/agents/:motebitId/succession` as the fallback and, whenever the relay is reachable, as the fork check. Refused ⇒ `Pairing refused: …` (a fork: `Pairing refused: identity fork detected …`) with nothing written (keyring/config, in-memory identity, private key and the relay device registration untouched), shown by the pairing UI. The result carries `relayCheck`.
- Device B persists what it verified: `openPairingKeyTransfer` returns the verified lineage (`succession`), and after acceptance each surface's `completePairing` merges it into its roster replica through one shared helper (`persistVerifiedLineage` in the private `@motebit/surface-kit`, which re-verifies — only a continuous, signature-verified chain to the held key is ever written — and merges idempotently). Before this, B verified the chain and dropped it, so when B (now holding the rotated key) approved the next device C, B's transfer carried no chain and the relay held none: C refused (`does not bind … no verified succession chain`) — a lockout of a current-key holder past the first hop. A → B → C now pairs for every identity kind (never-rotated, rotated online/offline/several times, `did:key`, legacy v7/v4).
- Restore from a motebit.md on web and mobile keeps the file's verified chain in the roster replica (`persistIdentityFileLineage`): web keeps no identity file and mobile's bootstrap regenerates it without the chain, so a device restored from an offline-rotated identity could never be Device A for it. (Desktop keeps the file itself.)
- The machine roster's identity-file rule (`boundIdentityFile`) now requires an intact file — signature AND succession chain — before a file contributes records or a guardian.

`@motebit/verifier` / `@motebit/verify`: `verifyArtifact` / `verifyFile` route an identity file through `identityVerifyOutcome` — a signature-valid `motebit.md` whose succession chain does not verify now reads `valid: false` with a `succession chain invalid: …` error, so `motebit-verify` prints `INVALID (identity)` and exits 1 instead of `VALID (identity)` / exit 0 (what `motebit verify` already said: `Succession: invalid`). A correctness fix to the verdict, not an API change: the pinned surface (`verifyArtifact`, `verifyFile`, `formatHuman`, `VerifyResultWithBinding`, `VerifyResult`) keeps its shape; only a forged chain's verdict changes, from a wrong VALID to INVALID.

`motebit verify <motebit.md>`, `motebit verify <bundle dir>`, `motebit rotate` (pre-check and post-rotation self-check), `motebit run`/daemon identity load, `create-motebit verify` / `create-motebit rotate` (pre-check and self-check), desktop `verifyIdentityFile`, web/mobile `verifyMotebitMd`, and the shared `importIdentityFile` (restore preview on all three app surfaces) reject a broken succession chain instead of reporting the identity intact.

Residuals, stated rather than hidden:

- A legacy UUIDv7 commits to no key, so a relay can still name one for any transferred key (the key transfer is not authenticated against the relay, which knows the pairing code) — that rung stays `unverified`, as it reads everywhere else.
- A guardian-recovered identity is refused at pairing by design: no guardian key is pinned on the pairing device, and one carried by the pairing could be the relay's, which would let it forge the lineage. The error says so and points to restoring from the identity's motebit.md with its current recovery seed. Seed-only restore does not recover a rotated identity's id.
- An older Device A sends no chain; then a relay that withholds the chain can deny pairing to a rotated identity (availability, not integrity): it cannot make the device adopt a wrong id.
- The fork check is only as good as the relay's reachability and its honesty about what it holds. While the relay is unreachable (the fetch fails), a holder of a superseded key can fork: it signs `K_old → K_x`, seals that in the transfer, and Device B accepts `K_x` at `sovereign` with `relayCheck: "unreachable"`, because the transfer chain verifies and nothing it can see contradicts it. Likewise a relay that withholds the real later links (serves a prefix, or `[]`) hides the fork — indistinguishable on Device B from a rotation not yet uploaded. A relay cannot forge a fork (only verified records count), so the worst it can do is hide one or, by serving real conflicting records, deny pairing to a key that is in fact superseded or forked.
- With no relay configured on Device B (mobile without a sync URL), only the transfer's own records are checked (`relayCheck: "not_checked"`).
